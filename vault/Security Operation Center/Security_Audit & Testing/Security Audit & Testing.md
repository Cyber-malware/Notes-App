Security audit is systematic review conducted to evaluate and `analyze` an organizations institutional process, security policies and IT components.

### Types of Audit
####  Internal Audit
Type of audits were audit team and `org` are managed by inside resources.
#### External Audit: 
Types of audit where audit team and `org` are managed by outside the resources..

#### Certification Audit
Usually carried out by `Govt` agencies or national/International accredited `org`. It usually conducted according to `ISO` or `NIST`.
#### Stakeholder Audit
Sometime `org` may have stronger requirement by their stakeholders such as compliance with the NIST framework, or they may be sector-specific.
#### Independent Audit
Sometimes organisations have to be subject to an audit even though there is no requirement to do so by any authority or stakeholder.

### Audit Standards, Accreditation, Certification
Security audit is usually carried out under the guidance and authority of the relevant authorities/stakeholders.
#### Audit Standards
Security audit standards are documents that provide guidance to auditors in security audit process. Main audit standard is International Organization for Standardisation `ISO` and other such as `ISACA` and `SANS`. 
#### Accreditation
The process by which national/international authorities audit and certify the competence and authority of auditors.

### Audit Documents
**Audit Documents** are the records and paperwork used to **plan, perform, document, and report** an audit. They provide evidence that the audit was conducted properly and that its findings are supported.

- Audit Process Document (Internal Audit Procedure)
- Audit Plan
- Evidence of Auditor Competence
- Audit Checklist
- Audit Report

Audit steps may vary depending upon the purpose and scope of target.

#### Audit Planning
Audit planning generally clarifies the parameters that will be considered in the end-to-end audit process and that will affect the audit.

`Purpose and Scope` : The purpose of the audit should be clearly stated. In this context, the answers to the following questions should be clarified:

- What is the purpose of the audit? Is it for general control or certification?
- Which management system or standard is being audited?
- Which sites, and processes will be audited?
- What services will be audited?
- Which units will be audited?
- Which projects will be audited?

`Time:` The schedule and duration of the audit should be clearly defined, taking into account stakeholder agreement.

`Resource:` The availability of the necessary resources for the audit, in particular `labor` and costs, should be guaranteed.

`Method:` The audit method should be clearly defined.

- What kind of a checklist will the audit be based on?
- Will only interviews suffice or will active controls also be carried out?

`Coordination:` Coordination should be ensured between the relevant stakeholders, in particular the audit team and the audited party.

#### Kick-off
A kick-off meeting is held before the audit begins to review the audit plan, scope, and important details. The lead auditor explains the audit process and clarifies the responsibilities of both the audit team and the auditees.
### Audit Implementation 
#### General Rules
Audits must follow legal, ethical, confidentiality, and company policy requirements while adhering to the audit plan. Auditors should act professionally and objectively, conduct planned/unplanned interviews, and maintain respectful communication throughout the audit.
#### Sampling
In large audits, examining every system or control may not be practical. Therefore, auditors use sampling methods to evaluate a representative portion of systems, records, or controls.
#### Collecting Evidence
Auditors collect evidence to verify both compliance and non-compliance with requirements. All evidence should be documented, traceable, and properly retained with details such as dates, versions, and document references.
#### Risk Approach and Level of Findings
Audit findings are classified based on their impact and severity rather than simply being reported as issues. Findings are typically categorized as **major**, **minor**, or **observations** to help prioritize corrective actions.

`A major finding` is a condition that completely prevents the operation of the controls used as a reference in the audit. The auditee cannot obtain approval until these findings are closed.

`A minor finding` is a situation that negatively affects the operation but do not prevent the auditee from successfully concluding the audit or obtaining a certificate.

`Observations` are suggestions for improvement that will contribute to more effective operation.
#### Closing Meeting
This meeting aims to provide information on findings and closure methods at the end of the audit.
#### Audit Reporting
Whether audits are carried out for certification or internal control purposes, a report is expected at the end of the audit.
